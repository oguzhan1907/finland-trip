# Everyman's Rights - Paddling

## Summary

This Luontoon source explains Finland's everyman's rights as a practical permission framework for spending time in nature regardless of land ownership. The rights allow ordinary low-impact access such as walking, skiing, cycling, temporary camping where access is otherwise allowed, picking unprotected wild berries and mushrooms, swimming, boating, moving on frozen waterways, and hook-and-line or ice fishing.

The source is equally important for its limits. Everyman's rights do not allow campfires on someone else's land, disturbing privacy near homes, littering, damaging trees, off-road motor-vehicle driving, crossing gardens or cultivated fields, collecting dry or fallen wood, collecting moss or lichen, disturbing wildlife, interfering with land use, fishing or hunting without required permits, or letting dogs loose in several restricted situations. Protected areas are a separate operational category: national parks, strict nature reserves, seal reserves, old-growth forest reserves, herb-rich forests, and mire reserves can have different access, camping, route, and collection rules.

## Why It Matters

For the Finland roadtrip, everyman's rights support spontaneous nature stops, paddling, swimming, berry or mushroom picking, and temporary camping. They do not remove the need to check local destination rules. National parks and other protected areas may require visitors to stay on marked routes, use designated camping sites, avoid restricted zones, and follow destination-specific rules in the Nationalparks service.

## Key Points

- No landowner permission or fee is generally required for ordinary low-impact use under everyman's rights, but laws can grant, guide, or restrict the rights.
- Temporary camping is allowed only where access is otherwise allowed; camping too close to homes or in prohibited protected areas is outside the permission.
- Campfires are not included in everyman's rights on someone else's land.
- Motor vehicles are not allowed off-road under everyman's rights, making this a foot, bike, ski, horse, boat, and ice-access framework rather than a car-access framework.
- Protected areas can restrict access, camping, plant collection, insect collection, fishing, and soil or bedrock disturbance.
- In national parks, roaming is usually allowed except in restricted areas, but camping is only allowed at designated sites and some activity modes are usually route-limited.
- Strict nature reserves and seal reserves generally require permission from [[Entities/Metsähallitus]], with limited route exceptions and specific camping exceptions named for Kevo and Sompio Strict Nature Reserves.

## Important Claims Or Figures

- Dogs must not be let off leash between 1 March and 19 August.
- Dogs are not allowed at public beaches, children's playgrounds, maintained ski tracks, or sports fields.
- Hook-and-line fishing and ice fishing are generally included, but may be restricted in some water bodies such as rapids and migratory fish waters.
- In mire reserves, everyman's rights are usually unrestricted, but bird-nesting season access restrictions can still apply in some areas.

## Planning Implications

- Treat public access as a flexibility layer, not a booking or safety substitute.
- Add destination-rule checks before treating a national park, strict nature reserve, seal reserve, or smaller protected area as a camping stop.
- Keep campfire planning separate from everyman's rights; use the Luontoon campfire source and current wildfire warnings before assuming any open-fire option.
- If paddling, swimming, or camping becomes part of the route, check the destination page rather than relying on the generic right-to-roam rule.
- If traveling with a dog, the trip needs a separate rules check before beaches, sports areas, public recreation areas, and nature stops.
