# Travel Pace

Travel pace captures the balance between distance, recovery time, weather flexibility, and actual enjoyment. A roadtrip can look feasible on a map while still being too compressed if every day requires a long drive, a ferry, a check-in, and sightseeing. This concept should be used to evaluate whether the planned route is comfortable or too ambitious.

The first route-source batch shows why pace needs to be judged by route module, not just total trip length. [[Sources/Discover 5 Extraordinary Road Trips in Finland]] proposes internal route modules from 1 to 7 days, while [[Sources/The Archipelago Trail]] recommends at least 3-5 days for the full 250 km trail. These durations do not include the approach to [[Entities/Ruka]], the return to Helsinki, recovery time, weather buffers, or spontaneous detours.

The current planning frame removes international vehicle-entry time but adds a fixed mid-trip anchor: [[Entities/Solstice Festival]] with a house booked from 2026-06-17 through either 2026-06-21 or 2026-06-22. Pace should now be measured against three fixed points: car pickup on 2026-06-06, festival arrival before 2026-06-17, and car return on 2026-06-25. Because the festival runs late and may include After Hours events, post-festival driving should be treated as a recovery and contingency problem, not just a map-distance problem.

## Planning Signals

- Multiple one-night stays in a row increase friction.
- Ferry or border days need extra buffer.
- Two-night stays are useful around high-priority areas.
- Weather-sensitive plans should not be packed into a single fixed slot unless there is a backup.
- Ferry-linked archipelago stages should be paced by timetable slack, not only road distance.
- North Lapland should be evaluated as a strategic priority, not as an easy add-on.
- Compact southern modules can protect spontaneity if the trip needs more buffer.
- Outdoor accommodation and kayaking days need extra slack for gear, weather, access rules, drying time, and recovery.
- Packrafting days need extra slack for inflating/deflating, carrying gear, drying gear, and changing plans if wind makes open water a bad choice.
- The first post-festival day should stay simple until the exact house checkout date and fatigue risk are known.
